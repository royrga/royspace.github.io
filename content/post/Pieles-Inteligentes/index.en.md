## Introduction

A product not only solves problems and needs, it is also the result
of a social, economic, and environmental analysis of human beings that fosters
communication between the user and the object (product). To achieve this
communication, it is important for designers to consider the message they want to send and the desired user response; they must also
understand the principles of product semantics that will be used.

Product semantics uses colors, shapes, figures, and textures to
send clear messages through the product [^1]. Semantics, when
used correctly, makes the user feel more emotionally and
psychologically comfortable, allowing for emotional bonds to be established with
impersonal objects, in this case, with the product.

## Background

In the 1980s, Uri Friedländer emphasized the shift in design orientation from being analytical and rational to being sensitive and emotional [^2]. Uri Friedländer explained his research through the use of metaphors [^3]. Natural metaphors that represent forms, movements, or natural events.

It expresses and refers to elements or events in nature, capturing forms and proposing analogies. Through natural analogies,
new concepts and ideas can be generated. This analogical process provides stability to products and allows for an approach
to efficient solutions that exist in nature.

Biomimicry observes, analyzes, and attempts to imitate
nature, seeking solutions to existing technical problems.

Architecture and design are presented as major areas of opportunity for the implementation of biomimicry.

Some applications are [^4] buildings or structures
capable of interacting with the environment, having dynamic shapes, being intelligent, saving energy, implementing new materials, etc.
[^4] Dynamic structures are capable of modifying their original shape to another, or other pre-established shapes,
depending on the interaction with their environment and changes in it.
Through biomimicry, the
characteristics of a natural façade, the skin, can be analyzed. The
skin's main functions [^5] are to protect
living organisms within it from the environment and interact with it,
monitoring physical variables such as temperature and humidity, making them
possible to control.

## Methodology

The proposed solution was to create a link between the
system and its owner. It was mentioned in the previous chapter
that the semantics of the product is of utmost importance for the user
to develop bonds with the product, and that these bonds can be created by imitating
the functions of nature. The decision was made to use the
concepts of dynamic structure and smart skins
to improve various aspects of homemade greenhouses.

The project involved a structure that served
as the base of a greenhouse. To achieve a
dynamic structure, panels were placed around
the base. These panels have a geometry that allows them to expand or contract.
The actuator is a motor that is capable of
moving the panels, and to control this movement, a light-sensing circuit is used.
This circuit, based on the amount of light received,
decides whether the panels should expand or contract.

## Partial Results

During the course of the day, the module expands or contracts its panels to
control the amount of light, simulating a living being. This behavior
represents the concept of smart skins and also involves the use of product semantics based on the actions of nature. By imitating nature, the user, on a cognitive level, becomes aware of the greenhouse and the care it requires. Based on this result and the initial proposal,
the goal is to improve communication (product semantics) regarding the use of home greenhouses.
